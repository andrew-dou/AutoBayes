\code{zero\_one\_inflated\_beta}. As before, every agent wrote from brms's documentation, the published densities and

\paragraph{How close.} Measured with the gates' own comparisons on \code{main} after the last merge (\code{0fb9649}): all 133 ungated
cases pass, 35 of them new in Tier D. Over the Tier D cases the worst relative errors are $1.3\times10^{-9}$ in G2,
$9.7\times10^{-16}$ in G3, $5.3\times10^{-15}$ in predictions (\code{hurdle\_negbinomial\_varint}), $1.9\times10^{-15}$
between the JAX and PyTensor densities, and $1.1\times10^{-14}$ in the post-processing tables
(\code{gauss\_loo\_compare\_x}); G1's designs are exact. Their log densities are within $5\times10^{-16}$ of brms's except

$7.2\times10^{-9}$ (\code{gamma\_distributional\_shape}'s gradient). Every value is inside the unchanged tolerances:
$10^{-8}$ for log densities, $10^{-7}$ for gradients, $10^{-9}$ for predictions.

\section{What brms gets wrong}
\label{sec:brms}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{\code{hurdle\_negbinomial} response draws (D86).} brms 2.23's \code{posterior\_predict()} does not draw the
  positive part from the zero-truncated negative binomial. At a fixed point ($\mu = 0.5$, shape $0.5$, $\mathit{hu} = 0.05$,
  a fixed-parameter fit, $1.9\times10^{5}$ positive draws) 81.6\% of its positive draws are 1 where the distribution gives
  60.4\%, and their mean is 1.27 where it is 1.71; at $\mu = 3$, shape 2 the means are 3.15 and 3.57. brms's own
  \code{posterior\_epred()} reports the correct mean (1.6218 $= 0.95 \times 1.7071$), so its draws contradict its expected
  value. D03 found this and recorded it in \file{tests/zero\_hurdle\_probes}; I reproduced it independently, with another
  seed, against rejection sampling from R's own \code{rnbinom}. AutoBayes draws exactly (the Gamma--Poisson rate given a
  positive count in closed form, then a zero-truncated Poisson), checked against the mass function for shapes from
  $10^{-12}$ to $10^{8}$. I am fairly confident this is a bug in brms rather than a choice, since the draws disagree with
  brms's own expected value; I have not checked whether a later brms fixes it.
\item \textbf{Stan's \code{ordered\_probit\_lpmf} gradient (D88).} On \code{cumulative\_probit\_varint} brms's log density
  equals the 50-digit value to $3.6\times10^{-16}$, but its gradient is off by up to $6.5\times10^{-5}$ relative, at 173 of
  the 176 entries (with and without the Jacobian). \code{oracle/reference.py} now implements the cumulative probit model
  (Stan's ordered transform and its Jacobian, each row's Normal mass from the tail it lies in), and the case is accepted
  under P6 against it: AutoBayes is within $4\times10^{-15}$ of the reference.
\end{itemize}

\noindent Two numerical limits are ours, not brms's, and are documented: PyMC's \code{BetaBinomial} loses about
$\epsilon\,\phi\log\phi$ as the precision grows ($1.4\times10^{-7}$ at $\phi = 10^{8}$, where Stan's is within
$9\times10^{-9}$; D82), and PyTensor's digamma is about $1.5\times10^{-10}$ off for arguments between 1 and 12, which
reaches the shape gradients on the PyTensor path ($9.2\times10^{-10}$ on \code{weibull\_varint}, $9.4\times10^{-10}$ on
\code{hurdle\_gamma\_varint}, both inside the $10^{-7}$ tolerance). JAX's digamma is exact, and NumPyro samples on JAX.

\section{What combining the features found}
\label{sec:integration}

The eight features were written against \code{main}s that lacked one another. I merged them one at a time, rebasing each
onto the last, re-running the gate tool on the tree that would merge and the whole non-sampling suite: 3{,}061 tests
passed after D01, 3{,}174 after D06, 3{,}280 after D02, 3{,}345 after D07, 3{,}487 after D04, 3{,}725 after D03 and 3{,}734
after the probit link. What the combinations turned up:
\begin{itemize}
\item D06's post-processing refused the new ordinal families' thresholds correctly, but its tests listed the refusals
  they expected, and the monotonic-term test listed the families that refuse \code{mo()}; both were extended for D04.
\item D04's own suite had never run (its agent was stopped by your pause). Running it found a test that used
  \code{sratio} as an example of an unregistered family, and an error message that called every ordinal family
  ``the cumulative family''.
\item Offering the cumulative probit link made the \code{link=} refusal claim that the cumulative family takes the
  cloglog link; it now lists each multi-link family with its own links.
\item \code{ab.log\_lik}, \code{ab.bayes\_R2} and \code{ab.loo} raised an \code{AttributeError} on a multivariate model;
  they now refuse it by name (D06).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The pause.} When you paused the loop, four agents stopped mid-way. D02, D04 and D07 had finished implementing
and were running their final suites, so I committed their work as it stood and verified it myself, re-taking the
snapshot of every earlier case model (unchanged in all three). D03 was mid-implementation; a fresh agent reviewed the
uncommitted work before building on it and found four real problems: a sampler that hung for over 40 minutes at
shape $10^{-300}$, an underflow that voided the whole zero-inflated Beta likelihood at $|\eta| > 745$, a $+\infty$ log
density at an observed $y = 1$ where brms gives $-\infty$, and a divergence row that paraphrased brms's source, now
replaced by output-based evidence.

\section{How the claims were checked, and their limits}
\label{sec:verification}

\begin{itemize}
\item Each feature: the gate tool on the tree that merged; the full non-sampling suite on it; the adapter and gate changes
  read for weakening (all extensions: \code{cs()} matching, shrinkage parameters, \code{hypothesis} and
  \code{conditional\_effects} checks, G4 reading \code{expected} for every family with trials); every earlier case model
  unchanged (snapshots of \code{target\_identity}, \code{describe()}, names and coefficients).
\item Reproducibility: \code{oracle/regen.py check --all} regenerated all 134 frozen fixtures from R on \code{main} after the last
  merge and compared them with the committed files: all 134 identical. The four committed 50-digit references,
  the new cumulative probit one included, regenerate byte for byte (a harness test).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Limits I know of.} Nothing in the loop samples, so no Tier D model has been fitted; the hosted sampling tests
have not run on anything since C05. \code{conditional\_effects()} is checked against brms on the two frozen cases only;
on every other frozen model it takes it is checked for consistency with \code{predict()}, which G4 checks against
  brms.
D06's names for brms's per-response, per-component and per-category coefficients, quoted in D99, were not probed.

  \code{logistic\_normal});

  from Wood's published algorithms, keeping the tests against mgcv's output.
\item \textbf{CI.} Still refused for billing. Separately, GitHub notes that \code{ubuntu-latest} moves to Ubuntu 26 from
  19 October.
\item \textbf{Attribution.} The ArviZ \file{NOTICE} question from Tier C is still open.
\item \textbf{A first real fit.} A sampling run of each Tier D family on the GPU or the cluster would test what no gate
  can. It needs your approval for the compute.
\end{enumerate}
